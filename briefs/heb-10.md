# Brief: Hebrews 10

**Shared across all three versions.** Stages 1–2 of `WORKFLOW.md`. The three renderings
differ in how they express this; they must not differ in what it means (0002).

Text: SBLGNT (`sources/sblgnt/79-Heb-morphgnt.txt`, refs `1910 01–39`), collated against
NA28 via STEPBible TAGNT (0083). Hebrew of the quoted passages from
`sources/morphhb/wlc/`.

> **Out of sequence.** This chapter was translated on request while Mark was in
> progress, before any of Hebrews 1–9 exists. Everything ruled here is provisional
> *only* in the sense that Hebrews 1–9 may add earlier first occurrences — the rulings
> themselves are binding (0020). Output is `<version>/translation/HEB-10.usfm`, a
> chapter-level draft per `FORMAT.md`, to be merged into `HEB.usfm` when the book is
> done properly.

## Structure

| vv. | Move |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | The law is a shadow; repeated sacrifice proves it never finished the job |
| 5–10 | **Ps 40:7–9** — a body prepared, God's will done; the first abolished, the second set up |
| 11–14 | The standing priest against the seated Christ |
| 15–18 | **Jer 31:33–34** — the Spirit's own witness; where forgiveness is, offering stops |
| 19–25 | The three subjunctives: *let us draw near / hold fast / consider one another* |
| 26–31 | Warning: deliberate sin after knowing the truth |
| 32–39 | Remember the earlier days; **Isa 26:20 + Hab 2:3–4** — do not shrink back |

The chapter opens with γάρ: it is the conclusion of the argument begun at 8:1, not a
fresh start. Section headings must not make it look like one.

## The three quotations

### 1. Ps 40:7–9 at vv5–7 — *the* crux of the chapter

MT Ps 40:7 (`Ps.40.7`) reads **אָזְנַיִם כָּרִיתָ לִּי** — "ears you have dug for me".
Hebrews reads **σῶμα δὲ κατηρτίσω μοι** — "but a body you prepared for me", with the
Greek Old Testament. The author then builds vv10 and 14 on the word *body*: it is not
decoration, it is the hinge.

**Ruling:** translate Hebrews' Greek as it stands (0003 cuts both ways — we do not
correct the author toward the Hebrew any more than toward the ESV). **Footnote the
Hebrew in all three** (0014). This is exactly the case 0034 was decided for: a reader
comparing with a Danish Psalter will otherwise think the translation is faulty.

Other detail in the quotation:
- **θυσίαν καὶ προσφοράν** renders Hebrew **זֶבַח וּמִנְחָה**, slaughter-offering and
  grain-offering. The received Danish is "slagtoffer og afgrødeoffer".
- **περὶ ἁμαρτίας** (vv6, 8) is the Greek Old Testament's fixed phrase for the
  **sin offering** (חַטָּאָה), standing as a noun beside ὁλοκαυτώματα. Render
  "syndoffer", not "for synd" — at vv18 and 26 the same preposition *is* ordinary and
  is rendered "for synd".
- **ἐν κεφαλίδι βιβλίου** = בִּמְגִלַּת סֵפֶר, "in the scroll of the book". κεφαλίς is
  the knob on the scroll-rod, hence the roll itself.
- MT has "burnt offering and sin offering you have not **required**"; the Greek has
  "you did not **delight in**". Not separately footnoted — inside the same note.

### 2. Jer 31:33–34 at vv16–17

MT Jer 31:33 has **נְאֻם־יְהוָה**, so λέγει κύριος takes `\nd HERREN\nd*` (0033).
The author has already given this quotation in full at 8:8–12; here he abbreviates it
and re-purposes it, quoting the *end* of it (the forgiveness clause) as the load-bearing
part. v18 is his inference, not part of the quotation.

### 3. Isa 26:20 + Hab 2:3–4 at vv37–38 — a composite citation

- **ἔτι γὰρ μικρὸν ὅσον ὅσον** is Isa 26:20 (MT `Isa.26.20` חֲבִי כִמְעַט־רֶגַע).
- **ὁ ἐρχόμενος ἥξει καὶ οὐ χρονίσει** is Hab 2:3, but with the article: the Greek Old
  Testament's neuter "it will surely come" (the *vision*) has become a masculine
  **"the coming one"** — a person. Hebrews reads it messianically, and ἥξει picks up
  ἥκω from vv7 and 9. Do not neuter it back.
- **ὁ δὲ δίκαιός μου ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται** is Hab 2:4b. MT (`Hab.2.4`) has
  **וְצַדִּיק בֶּאֱמוּנָתוֹ יִחְיֶה**, "the righteous shall live by **his** faithfulness".
  Hebrews' μου attaches to δίκαιος: **"my righteous one"** — God speaking. Paul quotes
  the same half-verse *without* μου at Rom 1:17 and Gal 3:11, which is the form Danish
  readers know.
- Hebrews also **reverses the two halves of Hab 2:4**, putting the righteous-one line
  before the shrinking-back line, so that ὑποστείληται in v38 sets up ὑποστολή in v39.

**Ruling:** composite citation footnoted per 0034; the μου footnoted per 0014, naming
the familiar Pauline form. Set as poetry (`\q1`/`\q2`) in all three — the NLT charter
requires it and the other two do it in practice.

Ps 110:1 stands behind v13 (ὑποπόδιον τῶν ποδῶν αὐτοῦ) as allusion, not citation, and
carries no κύριος. **0079 is not engaged**; no `\nd`, footnote the reference only.

## Vocabulary the chapter forces us to settle

Hebrews is a cultic-vocabulary book and this chapter is where most of it lands. The
governing constraint is **0007**: the argument of vv1–18 is carried by three words
recurring against each other, and if a version loses the recurrence it loses the
argument, whatever its philosophy.

| Greek | vv. | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **προσφορά** | 5, 8, 10, 14, 18 | The chain that must not break. The offerings God did *not* want (5, 8) are replaced by the one offering of Christ's body (10, 14), which is why no offering remains (18). |
| **θυσία** | 1, 5, 8, 11, 12, 26 | Second chain: the sacrifices that never finish (1, 11) against the one sacrifice (12), and none left for the apostate (26). |
| **τελειόω** | 1, 14 | What the law could never do (1) is what the one offering did (14). |
| **ἁγιάζω** | 10, 14, 29 | Perfect at v10 (accomplished), present at v14 (ongoing) — both are wanted; do not level the tenses. |
| **εἰς τὸ διηνεκές** | 1, 12, 14 | One phrase, two senses: endless *repetition* (1) against permanent *validity* (12, 14). See the decision log. |
| **προσέρχομαι** | 1, 22 | "Draw near" — the book's word for approaching God. v22 is the answer to v1. |
| **παρρησία** | 19, 35 | Frames the exhortation: the confidence we *have* (19) is the confidence not to *throw away* (35). |
| **τὸ θέλημα τοῦ θεοῦ** | 7, 9, 10, 36 | Christ came to do God's will; the readers must do God's will. Same words. |
| **ἥκω / ὁ ἐρχόμενος** | 7, 9, 37 | "I have come" in the psalm; "the coming one will come" in Habakkuk. |
| **ὑποστέλλομαι / ὑποστολή** | 38, 39 | Verb then noun, back to back. Keep the link. |

Further terms, with the pairs a Danish translation can easily collapse:

- **συνείδησις** — v2 is *awareness* of sins (Greek idiom), v22 is a bad *conscience*.
  Two Danish words are correct here; the English versions split it too.
- **σῶμα** (5, 10, 22) vs **σάρξ** (20). Not interchangeable: v20 says the way runs
  through the curtain, "that is, his **flesh**". Do not render both "legeme"/"krop".
- **ἱερεὺς μέγας** (21) is not ἀρχιερεύς. Keep "stor præst" so the variation shows.
- **τὰ ἅγια** (19) — "the holy things/places"; in Hebrews the sanctuary, here the inner
  one. Footnote the literal.
- **ἀφαιρέω** (4) and **περιαιρέω** (11) are different verbs for taking sin away; the
  variation is the author's.
- **ἀνάμνησις** (3) is a *reminder*, not the liturgical "remembrance" of Luke 22:19.
- **πρόσφατος** (20) — "new", but the word's root sense is "freshly slaughtered", which
  next to αἷμα Ἰησοῦ in v19 is unlikely to be accidental. Footnote in the ESV-style.
- **παροξυσμός** (24) is a hard word — provocation, incitement (Acts 15:39 uses it of a
  quarrel). "Stir up" is right; "encourage" is too soft.
- **θεατρίζω** (33) — put on public display as in a theatre.
- **φωτίζω** (32) — "enlightened", already an established term for coming to faith in
  this book (6:4). Not a metaphor to be dissolved.

## Exegetical decisions the versions may not differ on (0002)

1. **v9, "the first" and "the second"** are the two orders of approach to God — the
   sacrificial system of the law, and the doing of God's will in Christ's body. Not two
   halves of the psalm. The ESV-style may leave the demonstratives bare and footnote;
   the other two may name what is meant. All three mean the same thing.
2. **v12, εἰς τὸ διηνεκές** can attach to the offering ("offered one sacrifice for all
   time") or to the sitting ("sat down for ever"). Both are ancient. Word order allows
   the Danish to leave it open in the ESV-style; the other two resolve it to the
   offering with ESV, NIV and NLT. Footnote the alternative once.
3. **v20, "through the curtain, that is, his flesh"** — the traditional construal
   equates curtain and flesh. 0013 takes it; the instrumental alternative ("through the
   curtain, by means of his flesh") goes in a footnote.
4. **v26, ἑκουσίως.** The background is the Torah's own distinction: sacrifice was
   provided for inadvertent sin (Lev 4), and none was provided for sin committed
   "with a high hand" (Num 15:30–31). The verse is not saying that a Christian who sins
   is beyond forgiveness; it is saying that someone who repudiates the one sacrifice has
   no other to go to. **Footnote the Numbers background in all three** — this is the
   most misread passage in the chapter, and 0014 exists for exactly this.
5. **v29, ἐν ᾧ ἡγιάσθη** — "by which **he** was sanctified" refers to the apostate, not
   to Christ. Traditional and grammatically easiest; no footnote.
6. **v25, τὴν ἡμέραν** is *the* Day — the day of the Lord's coming and of judgment, not
   a meeting day. Footnote.
7. **v39, περιποίησις ψυχῆς** — "the preserving of the soul/life". Do not sharpen into a
   statement about the soul's immortality; ψυχή here is the person's life.
