# Brief: Romans 3:21–26

**Shared across all three versions.** Stages 1–2 of `WORKFLOW.md`. The three
renderings differ in how they express this; they must not differ in what it means.

Text: SBLGNT (`sources/sblgnt/66-Ro-morphgnt.txt`, refs `0603 21–26`).

## Stage 1 — Philological brief

### Greek text

> ²¹ Νυνὶ δὲ χωρὶς νόμου δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ πεφανέρωται, μαρτυρουμένη ὑπὸ τοῦ νόμου καὶ τῶν προφητῶν,
> ²² δικαιοσύνη δὲ θεοῦ διὰ πίστεως Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ, εἰς πάντας τοὺς πιστεύοντας, οὐ γάρ ἐστιν διαστολή.
> ²³ πάντες γὰρ ἥμαρτον καὶ ὑστεροῦνται τῆς δόξης τοῦ θεοῦ,
> ²⁴ δικαιούμενοι δωρεὰν τῇ αὐτοῦ χάριτι διὰ τῆς ἀπολυτρώσεως τῆς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ·
> ²⁵ ὃν προέθετο ὁ θεὸς ἱλαστήριον διὰ πίστεως ἐν τῷ αὐτοῦ αἵματι εἰς ἔνδειξιν τῆς δικαιοσύνης αὐτοῦ διὰ τὴν πάρεσιν τῶν προγεγονότων ἁμαρτημάτων
> ²⁶ ἐν τῇ ἀνοχῇ τοῦ θεοῦ, πρὸς τὴν ἔνδειξιν τῆς δικαιοσύνης αὐτοῦ ἐν τῷ νῦν καιρῷ, εἰς τὸ εἶναι αὐτὸν δίκαιον καὶ δικαιοῦντα τὸν ἐκ πίστεως Ἰησοῦ.

### Words that carry weight

| Word | Parse | Range | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Νυνὶ δὲ** | adv + conj | "but now" | Emphatic form of νῦν. **Salvation-historical**, not logical — marks the turn of the ages, not "however". |
| **χωρὶς νόμου** | prep + gen, anarthrous | "apart from law" | Modifies the **verb** πεφανέρωται, not δικαιοσύνη. Anarthrous = law as principle/system. |
| **δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ** | nom sg f + gen | "righteousness of God" | **Ambiguous — see below.** |
| **πεφανέρωται** | perf pass ind 3s, φανερόω | "has been revealed/manifested" | Perfect: a past act with standing result. Passive — God is the unnamed agent. |
| **μαρτυρουμένη** | pres pass ptc nom sg f | "being attested/witnessed to" | Agrees with δικαιοσύνη. Present = ongoing testimony. |
| **τοῦ νόμου** (v21b) | gen sg m, **articular** | "the Law" | **Wordplay**: νόμος appears twice in one verse in two senses — anarthrous "law" as system, then articular "the Law" as Torah/Scripture alongside the Prophets. Worth preserving if possible. |
| **πίστεως Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ** | gen sg f + gen | "faith in / faithfulness of" | **The major ambiguity — see below.** |
| **διαστολή** | nom sg f | "distinction, difference" | Paul's point: no Jew/Gentile distinction (cf. 3:9, 10:12). |
| **ἥμαρτον** | aor act ind 3pl | "sinned" | Constative aorist — sums up the whole as one fact. |
| **ὑστεροῦνται** | pres mid/pass ind 3pl, ὑστερέω + gen | "fall short of, lack" | **Present tense against the aorist** — sinned (once, summed up) but *keep on* falling short. The tense shift is deliberate. |
| **δικαιούμενοι** | pres pass ptc nom pl m, δικαιόω | "being justified / declared righteous" | Forensic-declarative, not transformative. Loosely attached to πάντες — grammatically awkward, theologically the hinge. |
| **δωρεὰν** | adverb | "as a gift, gratis, for nothing" | Same word as "without cause" in John 15:25. Strongly *without payment*. |
| **ἀπολυτρώσεως** | gen sg f | "redemption, release by ransom" | Commercial/manumission metaphor underneath. |
| **ἱλαστήριον** | adj ASN used substantivally | **three live options** | **See below.** |
| **προέθετο** | aor mid ind 3s, προτίθημι | "set forth publicly" **or** "purposed beforehand" | Middle voice. Both senses attested; "publicly displayed" fits εἰς ἔνδειξιν better. |
| **πάρεσιν** | acc sg f | "passing over, letting go unpunished" | **Not ἄφεσις (forgiveness/remission).** Paul had the ordinary word available and chose a rarer one. Sins were *overlooked*, not yet dealt with. Do not translate as "forgiveness". |
| **προγεγονότων** | perf act ptc gen pl n | "previously committed" | |
| **ἀνοχῇ** | dat sg f | "forbearance, holding back" | Deliberate restraint, not indifference. |
| **δίκαιον καὶ δικαιοῦντα** | adj ASM + pres act ptc ASM | "just and justifying" | **The climax.** Same root, juxtaposed — the whole argument lands on this pair. Any rendering that breaks the shared root loses Paul's point. |

### Discourse shape

One long Greek sentence, vv. 21–26, hinged on repeated δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ (21, 22, 25,
26) and closing on the δίκαιος/δικαιόω wordplay. Two purpose clauses (εἰς ἔνδειξιν,
πρὸς τὴν ἔνδειξιν) run in parallel — *past* sins (v25) and the *present* time (v26).

## Stage 2 — Exegetical brief

Paul has spent 1:18–3:20 establishing universal guilt. **Νυνὶ δὲ** turns the corner:
God's righteousness is now disclosed *outside* the law-system, though the Scriptures
had pointed to it all along. It comes through faith, to everyone without distinction,
because everyone equally needs it. God declares people righteous as a free gift,
grounded in Christ's death, which he publicly set forth. That death solves a problem
in God's own record: he had been *passing over* sins without punishing them, which
put his justice in question. The cross demonstrates that he is simultaneously **just**
and **the one who justifies**.

### Ambiguities — classified

Per `WORKFLOW.md`, each is labelled by *kind*, because the charters treat the two
kinds oppositely.

**A. δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ (vv. 21, 22, 25, 26) — AMBIGUOUS IN SOURCE**
Three readings, all defensible: (i) an attribute — God's own justice; (ii) a status
*from* God, given to people; (iii) God's saving activity, his covenant faithfulness in
action. Verses 25–26 lean hard toward (i); verse 22 toward (ii). Paul may well be
exploiting the overlap.
→ *ESV-style*: preserve — render as a plain genitive, let the reader carry it.
→ *NIV/NLT-style*: may lean, but must not flatten all four occurrences the same way.

**B. πίστεως Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ (v22), ἐκ πίστεως Ἰησοῦ (v26) — AMBIGUOUS IN SOURCE**
Objective genitive ("faith **in** Jesus Christ") or subjective ("the **faithfulness
of** Jesus Christ"). ESV, NIV and NLT all take it objectively; a substantial body of
scholarship takes it subjectively. Note v22's immediate εἰς πάντας τοὺς πιστεύοντας
("for all who believe") — on the subjective reading this is not redundant but the
complement.
→ All three of ours follow their English counterparts and render objectively, **with a
footnote**. See the Danish note in the decisions log — Danish can preserve this
ambiguity more naturally than English can.

**C. ἱλαστήριον (v25) — AMBIGUOUS IN SOURCE, doctrinally loaded**
(i) *propitiation* — that which turns away wrath (personal object: God);
(ii) *expiation* — that which wipes away sin (impersonal object: sin);
(iii) *the mercy seat* — the kapporet, the atonement cover of the ark, which is what
ἱλαστήριον renders in the LXX (Ex 25:17–22; Lev 16). The article's absence and the
Day-of-Atonement background make (iii) a serious contender.
→ The wrath/expiation choice is exactly where the charters will diverge. Do not let
the Danish quietly decide it; log the decision.

**D. προέθετο (v25) — AMBIGUOUS IN SOURCE**
"Publicly displayed" vs "purposed in advance". εἰς ἔνδειξιν favours the former.
→ Lean public display; footnote the alternative.

**E. The δικαιούμενοι participle (v24) — CLEAR IN SOURCE, AWKWARD IN TARGET**
Its attachment is grammatically loose but the sense is not in doubt.
→ This is the kind NIV *resolves* (start a new clause); ESV may leave the looseness.

### Not ambiguous — do not "fix" these

- **πάρεσις is not forgiveness.** Rendering it "forgave" destroys the argument: the
  whole problem v25 raises is that God *hadn't* dealt with those sins yet.
- **The aorist/present shift in v23** (sinned / keep falling short) is deliberate.
- **δίκαιον καὶ δικαιοῦντα must stay a visible pair.** If the Danish uses unrelated
  words the passage loses its point in every version, however free.
