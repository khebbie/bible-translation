# Checks: Romans 3:21–26

Stage 5 of `WORKFLOW.md`. Back-translation done from the Danish alone, then diffed
against the philological brief. Findings below are **not** silently fixed — they are
reported so the charter editor (and a Danish ear) can rule.

## Mechanical checks — passed

**δίκαιον καὶ δικαιοῦντα (v26) — the climax pair.** The brief required the shared root
to stay visible in all three. It does:

| | rendering | root shared? |
|---|---|---|
| ESV-style | "han selv er **retfærdig** og **retfærdiggør**" | ✅ |
| NIV-style | "han selv er **retfærdig**, og … han gør den **retfærdig**" | ✅ |
| NLT-style | "han er selv **retfærdig**, og han erklærer … **retfærdigt**" | ✅ |

**πάρεσις not rendered as forgiveness.** All three use "gå ustraffet hen" / "båret over
med" and footnote it. ✅ The argument survives in every version.

**Variant at 3:22.** All three follow NA/UBS and footnote the TR/Byz addition. ✅

**Pronoun referent, v25.** ἐν τῷ αὐτοῦ αἵματι is *Christ's* blood, not God's. The
NIV-style clause "Ham fremstillede Gud som sonoffer ved **hans** blod" is correct —
Danish reflexive "sit" would have wrongly attached the blood to God. ✅ Worth a
standing check wherever God is subject and Christ is object.

## Findings

### 1. NLT-style loses the δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ hinge at vv. 21–22 — *needs a ruling*

The brief flagged that the passage is structurally hinged on δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ recurring
at 21, 22, 25, 26.

| | v21 | v22 | v25 | v26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESV-style | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| NIV-style | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| NLT-style | ❌ recast as "erklæret retfærdige" | ❌ same | ✅ | ✅ |

The NLT-style turns the noun into a verb, which its charter permits. But losing it at
both 21 *and* 22 means the Danish reader cannot see that Paul is circling one phrase.
Defensible; not obviously right. **Charter editor's call.**

### 2. ESV-style copied an ESV *interpretive* move instead of being literal (v25)

"διὰ πίστεως" is simply "through faith". Our ESV-style has **"til at modtages ved
tro"** ("to be received by faith") — which is what ESV English says, but ESV English is
expanding there. Our charter says essentially literal; following the English wording
rather than the Greek is the wrong instinct. **Recommend "ved tro".**

This is the failure mode to watch across the whole project: rendering the English
version rather than the Greek. Worth a standing rule.

### 3. NLT-style weakens μαρτυρουμένη and drops προέθετο's public sense

- v21 "har hele tiden **peget frem mod** den" — μαρτυρέω is *attest/bear witness*, not
  *predict*. NLT English makes the same move ("as was promised… long ago"), but it
  shifts the Law and Prophets from witnesses to forecasters.
- v25 "Gud **lod Jesus dø**" — the brief said to lean toward προέθετο as *publicly set
  forth* (εἰς ἔνδειξιν favours it). "Let him die" loses the display sense entirely.
  **Recommend "stillede Gud offentligt frem" or similar.**
- v24 "Det kunne han gøre, **fordi**…" makes a causal claim slightly stronger than
  διά + genitive (means). Minor.

### 4. Additions in the NLT-style, all charter-permitted but worth seeing listed

"en vej" (v21), "hele tiden" (v21), "uanset hvem vi er" (v22), "ingen af os" (v23).
None distort; all are the kind of expansion the NLT charter licenses. Listed so the
cumulative weight stays visible — four additions in three verses is near the ceiling.

## Danish-idiom calls needing a native ear

Per `WORKFLOW.md`, these turn on Danish rhythm and idiom, not on exegesis. I can
defend each, but I should not be the one to settle them.

1. **δικαιόω across the three versions.** Currently: ESV-style *retfærdiggøre*
   (traditional theological term), NIV-style *gøre retfærdig*, NLT-style *erklære
   retfærdig*. The considered alternative for NLT-style was **"frikende"** (acquit) —
   punchier, unmistakably forensic, excellent read aloud, and arguably the most
   natural Danish. It was rejected only because it breaks the shared root at v26.
   Is that the right trade?
2. **"uforskyldt" vs "ufortjent"** for δωρεάν. I used *uforskyldt* in the ESV-style
   deliberately — it is the older Danish theological word and echoes the 1931/1948
   tradition the ESV charter tells us to anchor in — and *ufortjent* in the other two.
   Does *uforskyldt* read as dignified or as archaic?
3. **"uden lov"** (v21, ESV-style) for χωρὶς νόμου. Literal and terse. "Uden om loven"
   is clearer Danish but less transparent. Which way does the ESV charter cut here?
4. **"kommer til kort over for Guds herlighed"** (v23) for ὑστεροῦνται. Idiomatic and
   accurate, but longer than the Greek. The 1992 Danish Bible has "har mistet
   herligheden fra Gud", which is a different exegesis (lost vs. fall short) — I did
   not follow it.
5. **"sonoffer"** for ἱλαστήριον in the ESV- and NIV-style. This commits to
   *propitiation* (wrath-averting). The brief flagged three live options. If you want
   the mercy-seat reading foregrounded instead, that changes the word.
