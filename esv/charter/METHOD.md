# ESV — Translation Charter (distilled)

Position on the spectrum: **essentially literal / formal equivalence.** The most
literal of the three.

Primary source: `esv-preface-esvorg.md` (full official preface, verbatim).

## Core principle

> "The ESV is an 'essentially literal' translation that seeks as far as possible to
> reproduce the precise wording of the original text and the personal style of each
> Bible writer. As such, its emphasis is on 'word-for-word' correspondence, at the
> same time taking full account of differences in grammar, syntax, and idiom between
> current literary English and the original languages."

The governing word is **transparency** — "letting the reader see as directly as
possible the structure and exact force of the original." The ESV explicitly accepts
reduced readability as the price. It is openly polemical against thought-for-thought,
arguing that it is "more inclined to reflect the interpretive views of the translator
and the influences of contemporary culture."

Self-description of the trade-off: **"as literal as possible" while maintaining
clarity of expression and literary excellence.**

## Operative rules

| Area | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Concordance** | Use the *same* target word for important recurring original words, "to the extent that plain English permits and the meaning in each case allows." |
| **OT in NT** | Render OT passages quoted in the NT so the correspondence is *visible*, as far as grammar and syntax allow. |
| **Theological vocabulary** | **Retain** it: grace, faith, justification, sanctification, redemption, regeneration, reconciliation, propitiation. Rationale: central to doctrine, and the Greek words were already technical terms in NT times. |
| **Connectives** | Normally **translate** the "and / but / for" chains even where they exceed target-language convention, so the flow of argument stays transparent. May vary with "also, however, now, so, then, thus" where that better expresses the linkage. |
| **Authorial style** | Preserve the stylistic variety between writers — exalted Genesis prose, Luke's elegance, John's profound simplicity, Paul's closely reasoned logic. |
| **Sentence division** | Punctuate, paragraph and split long sentences along "the path that seems to make the ongoing flow of thought clearest." |
| **Gender** | Render *literally* what is in the original. "Anyone" where there is no word for "man"; "people" where both sexes are meant. But **retain** "man/men" where a male meaning component is present, **retain** generic "he", **retain** "brothers" (*adelphoi*) and "sons" (*huioi*), with a recurring footnote where *adelphoi* includes women. |
| **YHWH** | LORD in small caps. *’adonay YHWH* → "the Lord GOD". *’elohim/’el/’eloah* → "God". |
| ***Christos*** | Always "Christ" — consistency and concordance maintained throughout. |
| ***‘ebed* / *doulos*** | Context-graded: "slave" where absolute ownership is in view (Rom 6), "bondservant" for limited servitude (1 Cor 7:21–24), "servant" where freedom is wide (John 4:51). Footnote the original. |
| **Deity pronouns** | **Not** capitalized — nothing corresponds in the manuscripts, and capitalizing is a mid-20th-century innovation absent from the KJV stream. |
| ***hinneh* / *idou*** | Retain "behold" — the weight and dignity are part of the meaning; do not drop it. |
| **Footnotes** | An *integral part of the translation*: textual variants, difficulties and how they were resolved, plus significant alternative readings. |

## Textual basis

BHS 5th ed. (1997); UBS5 (2014) and NA28 (2012). Notably conservative toward the
Masoretic Text: translate difficult Hebrew **as it stands** rather than emending or
reaching for a version reading. Departs from UBS/NA in a few NT cases.

## Lineage

Not a fresh translation — a revision in the Tyndale→KJV→RV→ASV→RSV stream, starting
from the **1971 RSV text**. Retaining "the depth of meaning and enduring quality of
language" of that tradition is an explicit goal.

## Notes for the Danish version

- The Danish tradition has its own equivalent stream (Christian III 1550 → 1871 →
  1931 → 1992). An ESV-style Danish should anchor in that register rather than
  inventing one — dignified, liturgical, readable aloud, deliberately a little
  conservative.
- Keep the Latinate theological vocabulary: *nåde, tro, retfærdiggørelse,
  helliggørelse, forløsning, genfødelse, forsoning, soning*. This is precisely where
  the NLT-style version must diverge.
- YHWH → **HERREN** (small caps), matching Danish convention; *’adonay YHWH* →
  "Herren HERREN" or "den Herre HERREN".
- *hinneh/idou* → **"se"** — retain it rather than dropping it.
- Danish tolerates paratactic "og … og …" better than English does, so the
  connective rule is *easier* to honour here. Keep the chains.
- Danish generic *han* and *brødre* correspond closely to the ESV's retention policy.
- Watch the V2 word-order constraint: Danish syntax will force reordering that
  English does not. Transparency to the original applies to *structure and force*,
  not to word sequence where the target grammar forbids it.
