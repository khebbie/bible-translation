# The three charters side by side

Full distillations in `esv/charter/METHOD.md`, `niv/charter/METHOD.md`,
`nlt/charter/METHOD.md`. This file exists to keep the three Danish versions
genuinely distinct — if a verse comes out the same in all three, at least two of them
have drifted off charter.

## The spectrum

```
formal ◄──────────────────────────────────────────────────────► functional
       ESV                     NIV                       NLT
   "essentially            "neither criterion        "closest natural
     literal"              prioritized above          equivalent of
                              the other"               the message"
```

## The one-line test each version applies

| | Governing question |
|---|---|
| **ESV** | Does the reader see the structure and exact force of the original? |
| **NIV** | How would the author have said this if writing in today's language for today's whole readership? |
| **NLT** | If this book were written today, how would the author have written it? |

## Where they deliberately disagree

| Issue | ESV | NIV | NLT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recurring key words | Same word, concordant | Contextual | Consistent only for non-theological terms; theological terms get a *wider* range |
| Latinate theology (*justification*) | **Retain** — doctrinally central | Retain, prefer transparent option | **Avoid** — "made right with God" |
| *Christos* | Always "Christ" | Contextual | "Messiah" for Jewish audience, "Christ" for Gentile |
| Connective chains (*and/but/for*) | Normally translate them all | Restructure freely | Restructure freely |
| Generic masculine | Retain generic "he", "brothers", "sons" | Inclusive "they", applied "not inflexibly" | Plural or 2nd person; "brothers and sisters" |
| Semitic idiom | Keep the idiom | Render naturally | Render naturally, or illuminate inline |
| *hinneh / idou* | "Behold" — retain | Contextual | Usually dissolved into the sentence |
| Weights, money, dates | Literal, footnote modern | Mixed | **Convert**, footnote literal |
| Metaphor | Keep | Keep unless obscure | Clarify where a typical reader would be confused |
| Sentence structure | Follow the original as far as grammar allows | "Frequent modifications" expected | Whatever yields natural, clear prose |

## Worked contrast — the shape to aim for

Luke 2:7, *kataluma*. ESV keeps "inn" territory and the sentence shape; the NIV
changed to "guest room" on lexical grounds and says so in its notes; the NLT would
additionally recast the sentence so a listener gets it on first hearing.

Exodus 4:14, "his heart will be glad when he sees you". ESV keeps the Semitic idiom;
the NIV explicitly gives "he will be glad to see you"; the NLT does the same and may
go further on surrounding structure.

## Shared ground — do not mistake these for differences

All three work from **the same textual base** (BHS + NA/UBS) and all three retain
masculine language for God without exception. All three treat footnotes as part of
the translation. Where the three English versions differ, it is nearly always
philosophy — not a different Hebrew or Greek text. Check `sources/STEPBible-Data`
TAGNT/TAHOT before attributing any difference to a variant.

## Danish-specific: what the charters do *not* settle

The English charters answer English problems. These need deciding for Danish before
translating, and the answer may differ per version:

1. **Register and the 1992 Bible.** How close does each version sit to the
   Danish authorized tradition? (ESV-style: close. NLT-style: deliberately not.)
2. **Units.** NLT-style converts — to **metric**, not to the NLT's American units.
3. ***De/du***. Danish must choose a second-person register the English charters
   never had to address. Modern Danish *du* throughout is the obvious default, but it
   is a real decision, especially for prayer and address to God.
4. **Compounding.** Danish compounds where English needs a phrase — this affects the
   ESV-style concordance rule most.
5. **V2 word order.** Forces reordering English does not. Transparency for the
   ESV-style version means structure and force, not word sequence.
6. **YHWH.** HERREN in small caps across all three; but *YHWH tseba'oth* splits —
   "Sebaot" (ESV-style) vs. translating the meaning, "Himlens hære" (NLT-style).
