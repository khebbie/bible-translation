# NIV — Translation Charter (distilled)

Position on the spectrum: **mediating / balanced.** Deliberately refuses to sit at
either pole.

Primary sources: `niv2011-preface.md` (official 2011 preface, verbatim) and
`cbt-2011-translation-notes.txt` — *Updating the New International Version of the
Bible: Notes from the Committee on Bible Translation*, the fullest statement of NIV
method that exists.

## Core principle

The NIV frames translation as two goods that must be held in equal tension:

- **hearing God's Word the way it was written** (transparency to the original), and
- **understanding it the way it was meant** (comprehensibility).

> "The NIV is founded on the belief that if hearing God's Word the way it was written
> and understanding it the way it was meant were the hallmarks of the original reading
> experience, then accuracy in translation demands that **neither one of these two
> criteria be prioritized above the other**."

That last clause is the whole charter. Where ESV resolves tension toward form and NLT
toward meaning, the NIV's rule is that *neither may win by default* — the balance is
struck afresh in every verse.

The operative test:

> "…to articulate God's unchanging Word **in the way the original authors might have
> said it if they had been speaking in English to the global English-speaking audience
> today**."

Stated priorities: **accuracy, clarity and literary quality**, in a text fit for
"public and private reading, evangelism, teaching, preaching, memorizing and
liturgical use."

## Operative rules

| Area | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Beyond word-for-word** | Explicitly goes past formal rendering: "accurate communication of the meaning of the biblical authors demands constant regard for varied contextual uses of words and idioms and for **frequent modifications in sentence structures**." |
| **Semitic idiom** | Render naturally where the literal form is not idiomatic. Ex 4:14 "his heart will be glad when he sees you" → "he will be glad to see you." |
| **Contemporary vocabulary** | Track live usage and retire words that have drifted. "Alien" → "foreigner" (ET changed the word). "Ankle chains" → "anklets". "Overweening pride" → "great arrogance". |
| **Clarity** | Resolve ambiguity in the target text that is *not* ambiguous in the original. Lev 4:19–20 "atonement for them" → "atonement for the community" (the original does not leave hanging whether the bulls or the people are meant). Gen 29:10 "Laban, his mother's brother" → "his uncle Laban". |
| **Follow scholarship** | Revise as lexicography advances: *kataluma* (Luke 2:7) = "guest room" not "inn"; *lēstai* (Mark 15:27) = "rebels" not "robbers"; *harpagmos* (Phil 2:6) = "something to be used to his own advantage". |
| **Gender** | Where the original indicates application to mixed groups, use inclusive forms — singular "they/their/them" for the generic singular. Applied "not inflexibly": weighed against how it sounds in each context. |
| **Section headings** | Inserted as a reader aid; explicitly **not** part of the text and **not** for oral reading. |
| **Footnotes** | Flag places where uncertainty remains, and mark departures from the Masoretic Text. |
| **Revision discipline** | ~95% of the 2011 text is identical to 1984. Change is incremental and must be *justified* under one of three headings: changes in the language, progress in scholarship, concern for clarity. |

## Textual basis

Masoretic Text in the latest *Biblia Hebraica*; Qere readings sometimes followed over
Ketiv without footnote. Consulted: DSS, Samaritan Pentateuch, LXX, Aquila, Symmachus,
Theodotion, Vulgate, Peshitta, Targums, Jerome's *Juxta Hebraica* for Psalms. Will
re-vowel against the Masoretic pointing, and in rare cases emends. NT is an eclectic
text on the latest NA/UBS, with the committee making its own variant choices.

## Governance

The **Committee on Bible Translation** is an independent, self-governing,
self-perpetuating body with *sole* responsibility for the text — deliberately
insulated from the publisher. Diverse by nationality and denomination, which the
preface treats as the structural safeguard against sectarian bias.

## Notes for the Danish version

- The Danish target is the analogue of the NIV's brief: what the biblical authors
  would have written **had they been writing in contemporary Danish for the whole
  Danish-speaking readership** — pan-regional, not Copenhagen-specific, and not
  tied to a single church tradition.
- This version's identity is defined *by contrast*: where the ESV-style Danish keeps
  the Hebrew/Greek shape and the NLT-style Danish restates the sense, the NIV-style
  Danish should be recognisably in between. If a rendering is indistinguishable from
  either sibling, it has drifted.
- Danish V2 word order and heavy compounding make "modifications in sentence
  structure" not just permitted but unavoidable — this charter licenses them.
- Retain theological vocabulary, but prefer the transparent Danish member of a pair
  where one exists and reads naturally.
- Danish generic *man* is a genuine asset for the gender rule — it is neutral and
  wholly idiomatic where English must reach for singular "they".
- Keep the three-reason discipline: every departure from a plain rendering should be
  attributable to language, scholarship, or clarity.
